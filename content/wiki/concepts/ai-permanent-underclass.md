# AI Permanent Underclass

## Definition

The "AI permanent underclass" is [[wiki/entities/dylan-patel]]'s term for the emerging stratification between those who actively leverage AI tokens to generate and capture economic value, and those who do not. Those in the second group will be structurally disadvantaged as models become more capable, because the productivity gap between users and non-users compounds over time — and the concentration of resources among power users accelerates.

> "If you don't use more tokens and generate the value from them and capture that value... you'll never escape the permanent underclass." — Dylan Patel

## How It Appears in This Wiki

This is a new concept introduced by the [[wiki/sources/dylan-patel-token-supply-demand]] source. It sits at the intersection of economics (token arbitrage), industrial organisation (model hoarding), and social dynamics (AI protest, backlash).

## The Three Failure Modes

Dylan identifies three distinct failure modes, all of which lead to permanent underclass status:

1. **Failing to use tokens at all**: Simply not adopting AI tools. The productivity gap is already large and widens exponentially.
2. **Using tokens in the lazy way**: Using AI to work 1 hour instead of 8 — capturing leisure rather than economic value. Still employed, but not compounding.
3. **Using tokens but not capturing value**: Generating more output but unable to monetize it — because you're in a job, not selling something.

The winning path: work 8 hours but do 8x the work, make 5x the money, do it before AI becomes table stakes.

## Model Hoarding as Accelerant

The risk compounds because frontier model access is becoming stratified. Elite firms (top banks, funds like Citadel with Ken Griffin–style cash) can pre-commit billions in token spend to get early exclusive model access. This gives them a compounding information and execution advantage over those waiting for public access. See [[wiki/concepts/tokenomics]].

## Broader Societal Implication

Dylan's view is not that AI creates unemployment per se, but that it creates a **rapid widening of returns to capital and attention**. The person who points tokens at the right idea and captures the output captures enormous value. The person who doesn't is left behind, increasingly unable to compete. This is different from historical automation stories because:
- The speed is much faster (months, not decades)
- The skill required (idea selection + monetisation) is different from prior technical skills
- The concentration happens at the model lab level too (not just end users)
