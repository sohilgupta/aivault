# AI Surveillance

## Definition

The application of AI to monitoring physical space and behaviour at scale — analysing camera feeds, audio, and other sensors to identify, track, and predict. Distinguished from pre-AI surveillance by real-time pattern recognition (e.g. identifying a vehicle by its markings, not just a plate) and by the capacity to fuse many sensor streams into a single searchable layer.

## How It Appears in This Wiki

Introduced via [[wiki/sources/marc-andreessen-jre-2501]] through two concrete US systems:

- **[[wiki/entities/flock]]** — AI over municipal/traffic cameras for real-time vehicle ID and tracking; an a16z portfolio company.
- **ShotSpotter** — acoustic gunshot triangulation via rooftop microphones (separate company, not a16z); enables faster police response and ambulance dispatch.

The episode frames the central tension: these systems demonstrably solve crimes, but cities (Austin, Chicago) have disabled them over civil-libertarian concerns (potential abuse by corrupt officials) and "woke"/disparate-impact arguments (automated enforcement falling more heavily on disadvantaged groups). Andreessen's counter is that violent-crime victims are disproportionately from those same groups, and that the real question is governance design — warrants, logging, penalties for abuse — not abandoning the capability.

## The Embodied / Robotic Extension

The episode connects surveillance to a near-future where AI is a "control layer" over robots, smart glasses (Meta Ray-Bans + neural wristband), and home devices — raising the prospect that the government (or, in the US-China framing, the CCP) could see "everything you do from inside your house." This links surveillance to [[wiki/concepts/ai-sovereignty]] and the values embedded in US vs. Chinese AI.

## Open Questions

- What concrete legal controls (warrant requirements, immutable logging, abuse penalties) actually constrain Flock-style systems? Raised but unresolved.
- Does the disparate-impact objection survive the observation that crime victims share the affected demographics?
- As robots and wearables proliferate, where is the line between assistant sensing and surveillance?
