# Capex Cycle Analysis

## Definition

Capex cycle analysis is the framework for understanding periods when a company is investing heavily in fixed assets (plant, equipment, infrastructure) at a rate that significantly exceeds current depreciation and free cash flow. These cycles are common in capital-intensive industries (manufacturing, infrastructure, utilities) and are critical to equity research because:

1. **Current profitability understates intrinsic earning power** — new assets haven't yet generated returns
2. **FCF is temporarily negative** — but this is investment, not deterioration
3. **Return ratios (ROE/ROCE) may appear to compress** — equity raised before new assets generate profit
4. **The "if" questions** — does the capex translate into returns? What is the return on incremental capital?

## The Investment Framework

A capex cycle typically goes through phases:

| Phase | Characteristics |
|-------|----------------|
| **Pre-capex** | High ROCE, good FCF, limited growth |
| **Capex ramp** | Rising investment, falling FCF, rising debt or equity raise |
| **Construction** | Negative FCF, margins under pressure from fixed cost pre-loading |
| **Ramp-up** | Assets online, revenue growing, margins recovering |
| **Maturity** | High asset utilisation, peak returns, high FCF |

The key question for investors: at what phase in the cycle does the market price the stock, and does the price correctly reflect the future returns from the capex?

## Key Metrics to Track

- **Capex / Depreciation ratio** — >2x indicates aggressive expansion
- **FCF = CFO − Capex** — deeply negative FCF in capex cycles is expected
- **Non-current assets growth** — indicator of asset stock being built
- **Revenue / Gross Assets ratio** — declining during capex; should recover post-ramp
- **ROCE trend** — should recover 2–4 years after capex commences
- **Contingent liabilities / commitments** — often signals order book that will monetise the new capacity

## Gokaldas Exports Case Study

[[wiki/entities/gokaldas-exports]] is a clear example:

- FY23–FY25: investing ₹280–460 Cr/year vs. ₹78–369 Cr of operating CFO
- Non-current assets: ₹469 Cr (FY23) → ₹1,439 Cr (FY25) — a 3x increase in two years
- ROCE: 23% (FY23) → 12% (FY25) — compressed due to asset base expansion pre-returns
- OPM: 13.5% peak → 8.3% (Q3FY26) — new plant commissioning costs likely drag
- Contingent commitments: ₹2,558 Cr (vs. revenue ₹2,576 Cr) — suggests full factory utilisation ahead

The critical unknown: **what returns will the new capacity generate?** If ROCE recovers to 18–20%, the stock is likely undervalued. If margins don't recover, the capex was value-destructive.
