# India Apparel Exports Sector

## Definition

India's apparel export sector encompasses companies that manufacture garments and textile goods primarily for export to global brands and retailers (H&M, Walmart, Zara, M&S, etc.). India is one of the world's largest apparel exporters after China and Bangladesh.

## Key Sector Characteristics

- **Labour-intensive manufacturing**: Garment making is human-intensive and sensitive to labour cost inflation.
- **Dollar-denominated revenues**: Most exports are priced in USD/EUR, creating natural currency hedges for import costs but also forex revenue exposure.
- **Customer concentration risk**: Large players often depend on a handful of global brands for a majority of revenue.
- **Order-book driven**: Revenue visibility comes from forward order commitments; lead times are typically 3–6 months.
- **Capex cycle**: Scaling requires significant investment in manufacturing capacity (plants, machinery, compliance with global ESG/sourcing standards).
- **China+1 tailwind**: Global brands have been diversifying sourcing away from China since 2019 (trade wars, COVID, geopolitical risk). India and Bangladesh are primary beneficiaries.

## Key Structural Tailwinds (as of 2026)

1. **China+1 strategy** — global brands actively diversifying supply chains
2. **Free trade agreements** — India-UK FTA negotiations and other trade deals lower tariff barriers
3. **PLI scheme** — Government Production-Linked Incentive for textiles supports capex
4. **Quality perception upgrade** — India moving up the value chain from basic garments to technical/premium apparel

## Key Structural Risks

1. **Bangladesh competition** — lower labour costs; government export subsidies
2. **Currency risk** — INR appreciation squeezes export margins
3. **ESG/compliance requirements** — global brands increasingly require factory certifications, audits, green credentials
4. **Working capital intensity** — long cash conversion cycles due to order-to-delivery timelines
