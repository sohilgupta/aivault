# He Built The Revenue Engines for Google, Facebook & Square — Gokul Rajaram

**Source type:** Podcast  
**Original:** [[raw/podcasts/He Built The Revenue Engines for Google, Facebook & Square]]  
**Published:** 2026-01-29  
**Host:** Patrick O'Shaughnessy (Invest Like The Best)  
**Ingested:** 2026-05-17

## Summary

Gokul Rajaram — veteran product operator at Google, Facebook/Meta, Square, and DoorDash, and prolific angel investor — gives a practitioner-level account of how AI is fundamentally changing software product development, team structures, and the durability of software businesses. This is a ground-level view from someone who has built the revenue engines of multiple trillion-dollar companies and is now watching AI reshape every assumption.

His most important conceptual contribution is the shift from **deterministic software** (if X then Y, always) to **non-deterministic software** (AI agents produce variable outputs for identical inputs). This changes who owns quality: PMs must now own evaluations (evals), not just specifications. The PM's job shifts from "write the PRD" to "define the why at the highest level and evaluate whether outputs are good."

A second major contribution is his taxonomy of AI application durability: what makes an AI application last more than one year in a world where the capability frontier moves every two months? His answers — network effects, financial control points, hardware, unique scarce assets, and system-of-record ownership — form a useful framework for evaluating AI startups.

His account of legacy software disruption is precise: seat-based pricing models (Zendesk) are most vulnerable (AI agents displace seats gradually, as a two-way door); data-long-half-life businesses (ERP, CRM records, payments) are more protected.

## Key Claims

- The distinct PM/designer/engineer roles are collapsing into a single bottoms-up building function; PMs must now write code and do prototypes.
- Agentic tools (Claude Code, Codex) are now resilient to failure — a video transcription tool that took a failed attempt 6 months ago was built in one hour in January 2026.
- Capability frontier moves every two months; anything planned 6 months ago is stale.
- PM:engineer ratio going from 1:3 to 1:20 and beyond.
- The most future-proof human skill is **judgment** — knowing which of infinite possible outputs actually matters.
- "AI slop" is the core risk: when you can build anything, you need judgment about what to build.
- System-of-record companies (Slack, Epic, Salesforce) are starting to block or charge for API access as AI agents threaten to "treat them as a dumb database."
- Five durability pillars: (1) network effects, (2) financial/money control, (3) hardware, (4) unique scarce asset, (5) essential system-of-record ownership.
- To displace a system of record, an AI company must first build a seamless migration tool — a 2-year engineering effort before visible differentiation.
- Every AI native company must now aim to own the entire platform, not just the workflow layer.
- Larry Page's philosophy: scale over revenue (100% of internet ads, not $10B). TPUs, Street View, Waymo = 10-year technology bets.
- Zuckerberg's superpower: intuitive feel for consumer engagement; invented "custom audiences" as an organic idea, not a product roadmap item.
