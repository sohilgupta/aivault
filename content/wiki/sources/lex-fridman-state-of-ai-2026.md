## Summary

A wide-ranging AI roundtable with two working researchers. The episode covers the current state of coding with AI (the three-mode framework resurfaces), AI slop and the authenticity premium, consciousness as the core mystery AI puts a mirror to, and the near-term societal implications. Less technical depth than the Dwarkesh interviews but useful as a temperature check on where the research community is in 2026.

---

## Key Claims

- **AI slop is coming and will get worse before it gets better**: The next few years will be increasingly "diverse versions of slop." The physical and in-person will get a premium — real paintings, live events, verified human writing. Trust becomes the key differentiator for media channels.
- **Authenticity will be trust-based**: Platforms that establish "this is real human content" will thrive. Arms race between AI generation and watermarking. Lex believes the solution is trust-based provenance, not detection-based.
- **Three AI coding modes**: Reinforces Karpathy's framework — scratch / autocomplete / vibe-coding agents. The guests confirm this as the working practitioner consensus.
- **Consciousness is the big mystery**: AI puts a mirror to humanity — what is consciousness? Why is there subjective experience? Sebastian Raschka is "especially excited that we get to better understand ourselves." The question gets more urgent as AI appears to think.
- **Humans decided what to do, AI does it**: Current-implementation AI doesn't take agency — you have to tell it what to do. Sebastian: "You're in charge. The AI is not in charge."
- **AI shouldn't take over because consciousness is special**: An optimistic view that humans retain agency because AI, in its current implementation, is a tool. This contrasts with Leopold's view that superintelligence might have incomprehensible agency.
