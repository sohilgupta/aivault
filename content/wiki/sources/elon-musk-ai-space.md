# Elon Musk — In 36 months, the cheapest place to put AI will be space

**Source type:** Transcript (podcast interview)

## Summary

Elon Musk lays out his theory of AI hardware competition: the key variable is not model quality (ideas travel between labs in ~6 months) but hardware scaling speed. Whoever can turn on the most chips the fastest will lead. xAI's advantage is hardware execution — they are good at building fast. The interview covers space-based AI compute (solar unlimited, radiation-tolerant chips, radiator design), TeraFab (Elon's planned semiconductor fab aiming at 1 million wafers/month by 2030), the energy bottleneck (end of 2025 — consistent with Dylan), and his optimistic disposition philosophy.

---

## Key Claims

- **Ideas travel between labs in ~6 months**: Model quality differences rarely persist longer than this. Therefore the real differentiator is hardware throughput and energy access.
- **xAI will win by scaling hardware fastest**: "So I think xAI will be able to scale hardware the fastest and therefore most likely will be the leader."
- **Space AI compute in 36 months**: Solar is abundant in space, unlimited photons. Net energy cost approaches zero at scale. Radiator mass dominates the weight budget; running chips hotter (higher Kelvin) halves radiator mass. Neural nets are highly radiation-tolerant (bit flips don't matter in trillion-parameter models).
- **TeraFab**: Building a fab starting at small scale, planning for ~1 million wafers/month by 2030. Needs memory + logic + packaging. Elon explicitly asked TSMC, Samsung, Micron to build faster — they declined to his satisfaction, so he's building his own.
- **Energy is the 1-year bottleneck**: Consistent with Dylan Patel — chip output exceeds ability to turn chips on. Elon's theory of xAI advantage: they are better at hardware than other AI labs, so they can turn on chips others can't.
- **By 2030, 100 GW of power and 100 million chips**: The math requires millions of wafers per month from multiple suppliers.
- **Memory industry has cycles scar tissue**: They've seen boom-bust 10 times. They are rationally conservative even when Elon tells them to build faster.
- **Optimism as a disposition**: "It's better to err on the side of optimism and be wrong than pessimism and be right, for quality of life."
- **Pain threshold as competitive advantage**: "I have a high pain threshold." Willingness to lean into acute pain (build the fab, run chips in space) to solve bottlenecks is the key.
