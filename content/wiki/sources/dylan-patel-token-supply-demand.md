## Summary

[[wiki/entities/dylan-patel]], founder of [[wiki/entities/semianalysis]], is interviewed by Patrick O'Shaughnessy on the supply and demand dynamics of AI tokens. The conversation opens with Dylan's own firm as a live case study: SemiAnalysis has gone from tens of thousands of dollars in annual AI spend to a $7M/year run rate on Claude alone — more than 25% of their salary expense — with a trajectory that, if continued linearly, would exceed total salary costs by year-end.

The core thesis is that **demand for tokens is explosive and structurally unlimited**, driven not by model costs falling (they are) but by entirely new use cases unlocking with each capability jump. Dylan frames the competitive logic starkly: token access is becoming a scarce resource; those who can arbitrage it into economic value will compound, those who cannot will fall into what he calls the "permanent underclass."

On the supply side, the picture is a cascading bottleneck across every layer of the AI stack — GPUs (H100 prices rising, useful life extending beyond 5 years), memory (DRAM doubling/tripling again, new capacity not arriving until 2028), logic fabs (TSMC capex possibly hitting $100B in 2028), and further upstream in wafer fab equipment, copper foil, optical components. Margins are expanding everywhere. The economic value these tokens unlock is growing faster than the infrastructure to serve them.

The interview closes with Dylan's prediction: within 3 months (from April 2026), there will be large-scale public protests against Anthropic and OpenAI — driven by job displacement fears, a public that doesn't understand AI, and poor PR choices by AI company leadership.

## Key Claims

- **SemiAnalysis token spend**: Went from ~$0 to $7M/year run rate on Claude; >25% of total salary spend, growing fast.
- **Anthropic's revenue trajectory**: From $9B ARR to $35–40B ARR; gross margins have expanded from a leaked ~30% to a floor of ~72%, likely higher.
- **Demand driver is use cases, not price**: Cost per token is falling rapidly (Deepseek = 1/600th of GPT-4 cost), but frontier model demand keeps rising because the frontier unlocks economically valuable new things.
- **Mythos (Claude's unreleased model)**: Described as the biggest model capability jump in 2 years. More expensive per token but more efficient overall; Anthropic selectively gave it to top banks for cybersecurity only.
- **Software-only singularity is a blip**: Once implementation is trivially cheap, physical world (robotics) becomes the next frontier. Expects real few-shot learning breakthroughs in robotics in 6–18 months.
- **Scaling laws still work**: Mythos is proof — more compute into model still yields capability jumps. Release cadence has compressed from 6 months to 2 months between major model versions.
- **H100 useful life is 7–8+ years**, not 5 as previously argued; clusters are being re-signed for 3–4 year renewals.
- **DRAM will double or triple again** from current elevated levels; new capacity doesn't arrive until 2028. No rationing possible — demand destruction via price is the only mechanism.
- **TSMC may spend $100B capex in 2028**; tail-whip effect on downstream equipment (ASML, Applied Materials, Lam Research, etc.) is severe and underappreciated.
- **CPUs are sold out**: Two reasons — reinforcement learning environments run on CPU (not GPU), and all the "slop code" generated by AI runs on CPU instances.
- **Model hoarding is beginning**: Elite firms (top banks, Citadel-class funds) will get early/exclusive access to frontier models; this concentrates returns.
- **The hardest thing to measure remains**: The phantom GDP created by token usage — economic value diffusing through the economy that doesn't show up in standard statistics.
- **3-month prediction**: Large-scale protests against Anthropic and OpenAI; AI is less popular than politicians per Pew survey.
