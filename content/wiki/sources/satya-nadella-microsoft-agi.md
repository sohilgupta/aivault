# Satya Nadella — How Microsoft Thinks About AGI

**Source type:** Transcript (podcast interview)  
**Host:** Dwarkesh Patel (with Patrick Collison in part)  
**Original:** [[raw/podcasts/Satya Nadella — How Microsoft thinks about AGI]]  
**Ingested:** 2026-04-25

---

## Summary

Satya Nadella discusses Microsoft's unique position: the world's largest enterprise cloud provider, deeply partnered with OpenAI, and dealing with AI sovereignty demands from every major government. The central insight is that **trust** — not model capability — may be the most important feature in the coming AI race. Countries need to believe that their AI provider will be there in 10 years, won't cut them off, and respects their data sovereignty. American companies, if they can maintain this trust, may have an advantage in the long run even against Chinese competition.

Satya also covers: the concentration risk and open source as a check on it; the bipolar US-China world and "rebuilding trust in the American tech stack"; enterprise AI diffusion (consistent with Sundar: 2027 inflection); and the difference between capability and diffusion as two separate curves.

---

## Key Claims

- **Trust is the most important feature**: "Can I trust you, the company, can I trust you, your country, and its institutions to be a long-term supplier? That may be the thing that wins the world." More important than model capability in many markets.
- **Sovereignty is a first-class requirement**: Microsoft is building sovereign clouds in France and Germany. Sovereign Services on Azure include key management, confidential computing, and confidential GPU computing (done with Nvidia).
- **Open source is structural check against concentration**: Countries won't allow one model to dominate globally. Open source ensures they can always move data and liquidity to another model. This prevents any single lab from achieving permanent lock-in at the nation-state level.
- **Concentration risk + sovereignty = market structure**: These are the two forces that will shape how AI gets deployed globally.
- **AI sovereignty analogy to semiconductors**: TSMC is just better than alternatives, but countries can't accept full dependency. TSMC Arizona is "not real sovereignty" — it's a scam in some sense. But nations will demand it anyway.
- **The bipolar world (US vs China)**: Microsoft has to respect both US policy (allocate more wafer starts to US fabs) and EU data centre boundaries. Microsoft frames this as a business requirement, not a constraint.
- **Enterprise diffusion is 2027**: Consistent with Sundar Pichai — non-engineering workflows will hit their AI inflection in 2027. Large orgs have hard barriers (identity, access, security, change management).
- **AI-native companies will have an advantage over incumbents**: Startups that can design workflows around AI from day one will move faster than Microsoft internally transforming. But the size of Microsoft's transformation means when it lands, it lands hard.
